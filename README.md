# Gladiator

Fighters built from bone, joint and muscle, fought in a physics simulation.

This is the proof of concept: the body model and the core physics, in a ring, a subway platform or the Colosseum.

## Playing

The home screen (`src/menu.js`) has four ways in, over a fight between two random gladiators:

- **Quick Battle.** *Prize Fight* (boxing): gloves and trunks in the ring, an unarmed opponent of a random style within a couple of kilos of your fighter. *Blood & Sand* (gladiator): the Colosseum, a random knight, samurai, gladiator or polearm fighter, any weight. Fight as a random fighter, or make one step by step: who they are, their build, how they live (training and eating, with weight, fat, bench and sprint shown as they settle), how they fight, what they wear, their face, then fight. The fighter stands beside each step as it changes. **Next opponent** brings on someone new.
- **Levels.** *Sekigahara* (1600): ten of the East in red against ten of the West in black and blue on an open battlefield — spears, naginata and katana in front, bows behind, each man's side on his lacquer and the banner on his back. *Pier 9* (a container port at night): four SWAT officers — two pistols, two batons — against six yakuza — four knives, a katana and a pistol — between stacked containers, under dim sodium lamps, by the black water. *Frye vs. Takayama* (Saitama Super Arena, 2002): two big men in a ring under stadium lights, one hand each on the back of the other's neck, trading bare-knuckle hooks face to face; no holding down, it ends standing. *Last Train* (the subway platform) and *Peasant Rebellion*: two lords in full plate and three foot soldiers (spear, bow, long sword) against twenty rebels with spears in a medieval market square (cobbles, timber-framed houses, the church spire over the roofs, stalls, a well, the market cross, a cart, bunting, a bright sky), the rebels made fresh each visit. Rebels are simple characters: the same simulation, drawn without the bone layer, fewer rings in the body, ink outlines only round the big shapes and shadows from the body alone.
- **Deadliest Warrior.** Any character against any other (or one you make): knight against samurai, sumo against hoplomachus. In the Colosseum when either is armed, the ring otherwise; **Rematch** for another go.
- **Sandbox.** Everything, as before: teams of 1 to 20 (added fighters are copies of the lead; the dice re-roll them as anyone), the full fighter builder, scene, layers and shading.

Every fight is watched by default; **Play red** takes control of the red lead. **☰ Menu** goes back home.

- **Physiology** (`src/physiology.js`). The player sets daily calories and exercise; body fat is an outcome. An energy-balance simulation (780 weekly steps, stored energy split along the Forbes curve, RMR from lean and fat mass, everyday cost by the kilogram, exercise by the hour) settles weight, lean and fat, held between BMI 10 and 100. Bone density (T-score), squat, bench, grip, 30 m sprint, arm girth and impact tolerance follow from curves calibrated against six reference profiles (`node tools/physiology.js` prints the comparison; `test/physiology.test.js` holds them within tolerance).
- **Body model** (`src/body.js`). Sex, height, frame, age and the settled composition become bone, muscle and fat for each body part. From those come the particle masses, the muscle force limits, the collision radii, punch masses, chin and fracture thresholds. Every coefficient is in config with its source beside it.
- **Physics** (`src/physics.js`). Each fighter is 15 particles held by XPBD distance constraints (bones rigid, torso braces compliant). Motors pull each particle towards a desired pose with a force cap taken from the muscle, so hand speed comes from the body. Punches are hand targets. Landed punches are momentum exchanges between effective masses, and head speed change accumulates into knockdowns. A knocked-down fighter's motors cut out and the body falls as a ragdoll.
- **Life** (`src/life.js`). Seeded idle motion: a rhythmic bounce, head and trunk sway, hands that drift in the guard. Feet stay planted and step when the stance drifts away from them.
- **Styles and moves** (`src/moves.js`). Boxing, Kickboxing, Street, Muay Thai, Sumo, Mix, Unskilled and the weapon styles, each with its own stance, idle rhythm, attacks, defences and pressure. Attacks: jab, cross, hook, uppercut, roundhouse, low kick, teep, knee, elbow, up-elbow, clinch, charge. Defences: guard, slip, roll, parry, lean back, check, step back.
- **Speed from the body.**
  - Muscle force scales with cross-section (mass^2/3).
  - Hill's force–velocity law caps how fast a limb can move. Its top speed grows with limb length and falls with muscle bulk (pennation).
  - The skeleton is sized by frame, so a thin fighter carries dead weight, and fat is mass with no force.
  - Light-to-middle builds come out fastest; `node tools/speed-curve.js jab cross roundhouse` prints the curves.
  - Damage stays: knockdowns make the next easier, head damage lowers the chin, a fighter is hurt for a while after getting up, and a blow too strong for the body that took it knocks out on the spot. The AI switches game plans (out-boxing, pressure, counter-punching, brawling) and now and then loads up a heavy attack.
  - Down or out is a ragdoll: no muscle drive, relaxed-tone damping, ligament limits (spine twist included), limbs kept out of the trunk, and rest once still. `node tools/falls-quality.js` measures rig flips, jitter and settling; `node tools/glitches.js` sweeps every style pairing over five body types; `node tools/falls.js` and `node tools/outcomes.js` say who goes down and how bouts end.
- **Fear and confidence** (`src/ai.js`). Each fighter sizes up the other's best shot against his own chin (`strikeThreat`), corrected by the clean shots actually exchanged. A man whose opponent hits weakly presses, throws more and covers up less; one facing a heavy hitter keeps his distance and defends more. `node tools/fear.js` compares bouts with it on and off.
- **Cadence and spacing** (`src/ai.js`). Every fighter alternates spells of moving (circling at his distance, throwing only into an opening) and of working (attacking, much of it in quick bursts). How long each lasts, how bursty and how mobile come from the style's `cadence`, the game plan, a temperament drawn for each fighter, and his nerve, so no two fight to the same rhythm. Facing a longer reach or a blade with nothing as long in hand, a fighter waits outside the other's reach, surges in for a burst, and retreats, and steps away from a blade rather than block it; evenly matched fighters keep their usual distance. `node tools/spacing.js` measures distance, time inside the other's reach, surges, time working and attacks in bursts.
- **Holding down** (`src/physics.js`, `tools/pins.js`). When the last man of a side is down, up to two opponents kneel over him, hands locked on his chest and hips, pressing with 60% of their weight; the grip pulls only as hard as the arm can. Held, he struggles to get up against it; if his trunk is still down after 3 s of hold, he is beaten, knocked out or not. The hold strikes nothing and adds no harm; weight and strength decide it (a heavyweight keeps a lightweight down, not the reverse).
- **Sumo and Mix.** Sumo pushes men off their feet: open-hand thrusts and two-handed drives (little harm, real push: while the palms are on the trunk the legs keep driving), the charge, and the grip with the legs walking a man back until his own legs cannot take it. Mix switches every ten seconds or so between boxing, kickboxing, Muay Thai and sumo (never a weapon style), and is the default for fighters added to a side.
- **Swordsmanship.** A fighter with the longer weapon holds just outside the other's reach and inside his own, gives ground when pressed, and meets whoever steps in with a quick burst of chained cuts. Blocks go to meet the strike: the blade is set across its line and driven out into it, so blades clash, and a fist or a shin that comes on meets the edge, is stopped, and is cut as deep as it drove in — a hand can come off on a blade. `node tools/swords.js` measures range, tempo, blocks and hands lost.
- **Headgear.** One piece at a time, from what the outfit allows: a headset (any outfit without a helmet), a runner's cap, a hiker's boonie hat, a yakuza's hat, a commoner's head wrap, a knight's plume, a samurai's crest (on by default). Each comes off when a blow moves the head hard enough (a headset at any clean shot, a hat at about 1.1 m/s, a crest only at 2.8); hats also come off in a fall. `designSheet('headgear')` shows them all.
- **Polearms.** War hammer (a heavy head that drives through armour: a clean head shot knocks out a man in plate), naginata (long, curved, cuts deep, sweeps through more than one), and spear (long, thrusting, keeps backing off to strike from the point; the easiest to tear from the hands). All hold the range their reach gives them and fight mixed when disarmed.
- **Bow.** Held in the left hand, drawn to the cheek and loosed; arrows fly and drop, aimed high for the distance, and strike whatever is first on their path. Mostly a piercing wound with a small knock; they glance off full plate, tōsei gusoku and ō-yoroi nine times in ten. Closed with, the archer drops the bow for his sidearm.
- **Sidearms.** Knight armour carries a dagger (plate) or a short sword (mail, foot soldier); samurai armour a wakizashi. Losing the main weapon, he draws it, once.
- **Play either side.** Play red or Play blue takes the side's lead.
- **Deadliest Warrior** is two turning carousels of portraits, red above and blue below, covering every style and kind of armour; swipe or press the arrows.
- **Handgun.** A modern pistol (`WEAPONS.pistol`, `GUN`): the gunman keeps his distance, raises the gun, aims through the sights and fires when they settle; the round goes down that line, give or take the aim's error (more moving or reeling). One hit to the head kills an unarmoured 80 kg man, two to the body, five to the arms and legs; a heavier part takes less, a hurt one more. Armour takes its share (`protection.bullet`): SWAT 60% head, 90% body, 50% limbs; knight 40/70/30; samurai 30/40/10; hoplomachus 30/0/10; a shield stops it. A hit to the head or body staggers anyone at once, and every wound bleeds. Engaging, he stands in the Chapman stance — bladed, feet staggered, knees bent, weight forward, the gun arm locked straight and the support hand wrapped round from below — and backs away as he fires. If a man is coming too fast for a shot, he turns and runs for the most open ground (never into a corner) for a moment, then turns and shoots again. Once a man is on him (1.2 m) he drops the gun and fights mixed, and picks it up again only with room to use it; the gun is also very easily knocked from the hand. Anyone facing a gun runs at him flat out, weaving, and charges; gunfights start further apart. Rounds off a knight's plate ricochet in sparks; on samurai and SWAT armour they puff; neither bleeds. Sgt. Dana Cole is armed SWAT. `node tools/gun.js`.
- **Clinch brawler.** One hand clamped on the back of the neck (`collarTie`), the other hammering hooks and uppercuts; grabbed, he grabs back.
- **Armour types** (`src/outfits.js`, pieces in `ARMOR_KINDS`, `src/loftbody.js`). Samurai: tōsei gusoku (riveted steel cuirass, war mask; 75/95/88% blunt/cut/pierce), ō-yoroi (laced lamellar, leather front panel; 70/90/80), ashigaru (lacquered okegawa-do and jingasa; 50/70/55, lighter). Knights: full plate (60/100/90, blades glance), mail and great helm under a heraldic surcoat (45/85/50), foot soldier in brigandine and kettle hat (50/75/60). Gladiators: hoplomachus (40/40/20), murmillo (fin-crested helmet; 45/50/30), secutor (smooth egg helmet; 50/50/35), retiarius (galerus only; 15/20/10, fastest), thraex (griffin helmet, greaves on both legs; 40/45/25). Each has five designs; `picked` keeps the ones in play, and fighters the game makes wear one of them at random (`redress`). `designSheet('outfit-<kind>')` shows them.
- **Fear, panic and adrenaline** (`src/ai.js`, cheap: once a think and from events already read). Fear (0–1) settles over a second or two towards half of feeling outmatched plus most of how hurt he is (brain strain, blood lost, gunshot harm, knockdowns), less his armour's courage (knight 35%, samurai 30%, gladiator 25%: they also charge more). Past 0.7 he may break: he throws down his weapon and turns passive — covers up and runs from whoever is nearest — until he is calm for a few seconds. Adrenaline (0–1) surges on putting a man down or surviving a very hard blow, more in young, big and male bodies, less with age, and fades over half a minute; while it is high he will not break, and it drowns out fear. **Passive** is also a style of its own: chosen in the sandbox, the fighter never fights back. `node tools/panic.js`.
- **Stagger.** Armour that stops half of blunt harm or more (knight, samurai, SWAT) spreads a heavy blow: the man reels instead of dropping. A heavy blunt blow (by its peak force, so a war hammer, not a cut), a hard head shot, or being driven past his feet just after a hit staggers him for 2 to 10 s, longer the harder it was; one that would knock him down only staggers him, unless it is overwhelming or he is already reeling. Reeling, he is weaker and slower, his blows carry less, and he defends half as often, all coming back as it wears off; opponents press. `node tools/stagger.js` measures it.
- **Arms held forward.** Weapon arms bend down and out, not back, with the hand ahead of the shoulder; polearms are held front hand ahead on the shaft, the shaft sliding through it. `node tools/arms.js` measures how often an elbow falls behind the shoulder.
- **Fear of reach.** Nerve drops with every metre of reach the other has; facing it, a fighter holds off and surges in at openings — a blow being recovered from, a step back, a weapon off the line.
- **Characters and teams.** Every style has a character dressed for it (Sir Edric the knight, Takeda the samurai, Tomoe with her naginata, …). Fighters added to a side are the lead again, varied in build, age, looks and sex; the dice beside a side's size re-roll them as anyone.
- **Losing and finding weapons.** A weapon is hard to lose: the grip takes about twice the jarring it did, and a fall shakes it loose only now and then (a knockout always). A fighter who loses his weapon fights mixed (the hoplomachus draws his gladius first). Anyone without a weapon — its owner or an opponent — goes for one on the floor when it is nearer him than them and no blow is coming: walks to it, stoops, and fights on in that weapon's style.
- **Unskilled.** An ordinary person: square, hands low, wild hooks and crosses one at a time, poor technique (less body behind each blow) and aim, no blocking or slipping — only a late flinch back.
- **Colours.** Each outfit has a palette of combinations that suit it; fighters spawn with one at random, and **Shuffle colours** (per fighter) or **Shuffle all colours** rolls again. The Commoner outfit is a medieval common man: belted tunic and hose, barefoot, no traits.
- **Toughness.** One factor (`BODY.toughness`, 0.55) sets how much harm everyone takes before it tells: chin, brain strain, face and rib fractures, tissue damage, legs giving way, the whole-body knockout, cutting through, killing stabs and blood loss. Lower ends fights sooner with fists and weapons alike; the physics is untouched.
- **Team fights**, 1 to 8 a side and not necessarily even. Each fighter keeps a focus: the man in front of him, the nearest one standing once his man is down, at once whoever hits him instead. Sides line up in rows that fit the floor. Gangs take turns (at most three on one man; the rest circle just out of range), numbers count for confidence, and blows from outside a man's field of view land harder (blindside). Team-mates spread out, sidestep to keep the line clear, hold a punch while a team-mate is in or just past it, and keep kicks for when nobody stands beside them: friendly fire under 2%. `node tools/crowds.js` measures speed, outcomes, friendly fire and glitches. In the builder every fighter on both sides can be picked and edited; **Copy setup** / **Load setup** keep the whole fight (level, sides, every fighter) as JSON.
- **Levels** (`src/scenarios.js`). Set fights in set places, on the same world and physics: the subway level puts two men in street clothes, bare-fisted, on a narrow platform. Bare fists peak harder and break hands and cut sooner; clothes are lofts weighted like the skin under them; hair, hoods and drawstrings swing as pendulums that rest on the body (`src/dangle.js`); a headset is a prop knocked off by the first clean head shot.
- **Outfits** (`src/outfits.js`, drawn by `src/wardrobe.js`). Boxing, Sports, Sumo, Hiking, Casual, Business, Yakuza, SWAT, Knight armour, Samurai armour (red lacquered lamellar, five designs), Hoplomachus and Commoner. Traits are physics or harm, kept apart: movement (footwork, acceleration, limb speed), balance, kick weight, gear mass (real mass: knockback follows it), and protection that cuts harm by kind, never the impulse:

  | Armour | Blunt | Cut | Pierce | Weight |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | SWAT | 80% | 60% | 40% | +20% |
  | Knight (blades glance off) | 60% | 100% | 90% | +50% |
  | Samurai | 70% | 90% | 80% | +40% |
  | Hoplomachus | 40% | 40% | 20% | +30% |

  Steel is a separate metal mesh lit by one painted reflection map; lacquered lamellar is less metallic, so its red shows.
- **Weapons** (`src/weapons.js`, drawn by `src/weaponview.js`). Police baton, long sword (hand-and-a-half), katana (two hands, held upright), knife, and the hoplomachus's spear with a parma shield and a gladius in reserve. A weapon is a rigid line from the main hand; a wrist spring turns it as fast as its inertia allows (stiffer with two hands, the off hand drawn onto the grip), and its mass rides on the hands. Weapon moves follow a path through the target. On contact the impulse is a collision of effective masses — a free body, or arm and weapon locked and turning about the shoulder for a cut, or pushing as one for a thrust — and the harm splits by how the contact moved: an edge drawn across cuts, a point arriving first pierces, and the rest is blunt. Cuts bleed and, deep enough near a joint, take the part off (the skin above it is lifted into a loose piece; the stump bleeds in beats; blood stains the floor); a deep stab into the chest or head kills; blood loss collapses a man. Plate turns blades aside; a shield stops what meets it; blades clash; hard blows to the weapon arm or the weapon jar the grip loose, and a man knocked down drops it — then he boxes (the hoplomachus draws his gladius). `node tools/weapon-strikes.js katana` throws each move at a standing man from set distances; `node tools/weapons.js` plays weapon bouts and tallies how they end; `node tools/glitches.js weapons` sweeps them for glitches.
- **Places.** The ring, the subway platform, the Colosseum (sand, the podium wall and its gates, the stepped cavea full of people, open sky and sun) the village green (grass, cottages, a fence, haystacks), the stadium and the town square can be picked in the sandbox.
- **Big moments** (`src/drama.js`). Knockouts, severed parts, killing stabs and knockdowns slow the simulation, push the camera in and shake it; icons pop over KOs, cuts through, kills, blood loss, disarms, knockdowns, broken joints and lost gear.
  - Boxers fight in combinations at a volume puncher's pace, and defend by reading the strike: after a reaction time training shortens, the head goes off the punch's line (outside the punching arm, under a hook), and it weaves off the centre line whenever in range. `node tools/aggression.js boxing 16 60` prints punch rate, combination share and how many head shots land clean.
- **Body contact.** Collisions share momentum by mass. A charge transfers its momentum. Blows put a "knock" into the trunk that the legs must absorb, and too much topples a fighter. The clinch locks hands to the opponent's neck with constraints. Leg kicks accumulate damage until the leg gives way.
- **Injuries.** A blow hands over more momentum than its cushioned damage figure, for a sharp knockback. Knees and elbows are hinges, hips and the neck are cones. A joint held well past its range (strain = angle past the limit × time, leaking away) breaks: its limb goes limp and turns red, and a broken knee, hip or neck ends the fight. Each body segment accumulates damage from the blows it takes, and the skin reddens with it.
- **Fight AI** (`src/ai.js`). The AI issues the same commands the player does. It chooses moves by distance and defences by what is coming, and its style sets how often it presses inside or charges.
- **Hits.** A landed punch gives its momentum to the struck part, and the punching arm takes the same momentum back. The struck muscles hold only passive tone for a reflex delay of 55–85 ms, then catch the part with whatever force they have. Muscles are spring-dampers capped by their strength, so every movement has inertia. Knees, elbows and the neck have joint limits for the ragdoll.
- **Face** (`src/face.js`). Lightly anime: large drawn eyes (white, two-tone iris, pupil, highlights, a heavy lash line), a small nose and mouth, and a soft V-shaped jaw. Hair is built from tapered locks (spiky, short, fade, buzz, cornrows, bun, ponytail), and there is optional facial hair. It winces when hit, goes slack when stunned, shuts when knocked out, pants when tired, and reddens with damage.
- **Rig and skin** (`src/rig.js`, `src/bodymesh.js`). Seventeen bones, whose frames come straight from the physics particles. The body is one continuous skinned mesh, generated from that fighter's anatomy: each muscle group and fat deposit is a rounded shape, blended into the next with a smooth minimum. The mesh is built in an A-pose (surface nets) and each vertex is weighted to its nearest bones. A limb's bones only move skin on their own side of the body.
- **Skeleton** (`src/bones.js`). Skull with jaw and eye sockets, vertebrae, ten pairs of ribs and a sternum, shoulder blades, clavicles, pelvis, humerus, radius and ulna, femur and kneecap, tibia and fibula, and foot bones. Each piece rides its rig bone.
- **Style** (`src/toon.js`). Cel shading with ink outlines by default, which keeps the stylised faces out of the uncanny valley. Soft lit shading is available to compare.
- **View** (`src/render.js`). Three.js renders four layers: skin, muscle, bone and the physics skeleton. The skin is a soft shell of damped springs, so punches dent it.

## Run

```sh
npm run serve        # http://127.0.0.1:8934
npm test
npm run bout -- heavy:muayThai light:kickboxing 10 180   # headless bouts by preset:style
npm run bundle       # dist/gladiator.html, one self-contained page
```
