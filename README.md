# Gladiator

Fighters built from bone, joint and muscle, fought in a physics simulation.

This is the proof of concept: the body model and the core physics, in a ring, a subway platform or the Colosseum.

- **Physiology** (`src/physiology.js`). The player sets daily calories and exercise; body fat is an outcome. An energy-balance simulation (780 weekly steps, stored energy split along the Forbes curve, RMR from lean and fat mass, everyday cost by the kilogram, exercise by the hour) settles weight, lean and fat, held between BMI 10 and 100. Bone density (T-score), squat, bench, grip, 30 m sprint, arm girth and impact tolerance follow from curves calibrated against six reference profiles (`node tools/physiology.js` prints the comparison; `test/physiology.test.js` holds them within tolerance).
- **Body model** (`src/body.js`). Sex, height, frame, age and the settled composition become bone, muscle and fat for each body part. From those come the particle masses, the muscle force limits, the collision radii, punch masses, chin and fracture thresholds. Every coefficient is in config with its source beside it.
- **Physics** (`src/physics.js`). Each fighter is 15 particles held by XPBD distance constraints (bones rigid, torso braces compliant). Motors pull each particle towards a desired pose with a force cap taken from the muscle, so hand speed comes from the body. Punches are hand targets. Landed punches are momentum exchanges between effective masses, and head speed change accumulates into knockdowns. A knocked-down fighter's motors cut out and the body falls as a ragdoll.
- **Life** (`src/life.js`). Seeded idle motion: a rhythmic bounce, head and trunk sway, hands that drift in the guard. Feet stay planted and step when the stance drifts away from them.
- **Styles and moves** (`src/moves.js`). Boxing, Kickboxing, Street, Muay Thai and the weapon styles, each with its own stance, idle rhythm, attacks, defences and pressure. Attacks: jab, cross, hook, uppercut, roundhouse, low kick, teep, knee, elbow, up-elbow, clinch, charge. Defences: guard, slip, roll, parry, lean back, check, step back.
- **Speed from the body.**
  - Muscle force scales with cross-section (mass^2/3).
  - Hill's force–velocity law caps how fast a limb can move. Its top speed grows with limb length and falls with muscle bulk (pennation).
  - The skeleton is sized by frame, so a thin fighter carries dead weight, and fat is mass with no force.
  - Light-to-middle builds come out fastest; `node tools/speed-curve.js jab cross roundhouse` prints the curves.
  - Damage stays: knockdowns make the next easier, head damage lowers the chin, a fighter is hurt for a while after getting up, and a blow too strong for the body that took it knocks out on the spot. The AI switches game plans (out-boxing, pressure, counter-punching, brawling) and now and then loads up a heavy attack.
  - Down or out is a ragdoll: no muscle drive, relaxed-tone damping, ligament limits (spine twist included), limbs kept out of the trunk, and rest once still. `node tools/falls-quality.js` measures rig flips, jitter and settling; `node tools/glitches.js` sweeps every style pairing over five body types; `node tools/falls.js` and `node tools/outcomes.js` say who goes down and how bouts end.
- **Fear and confidence** (`src/ai.js`). Each fighter sizes up the other's best shot against his own chin (`strikeThreat`), corrected by the clean shots actually exchanged. A man whose opponent hits weakly presses, throws more and covers up less; one facing a heavy hitter keeps his distance and defends more. `node tools/fear.js` compares bouts with it on and off.
- **Cadence and spacing** (`src/ai.js`). Every fighter alternates spells of moving (circling at his distance, throwing only into an opening) and of working (attacking, much of it in quick bursts). How long each lasts, how bursty and how mobile come from the style's `cadence`, the game plan, a temperament drawn for each fighter, and his nerve, so no two fight to the same rhythm. Facing a longer reach or a blade with nothing as long in hand, a fighter waits outside the other's reach, surges in for a burst, and retreats, and steps away from a blade rather than block it; evenly matched fighters keep their usual distance. `node tools/spacing.js` measures distance, time inside the other's reach, surges, time working and attacks in bursts.
- **Toughness.** One factor (`BODY.toughness`, 0.7) sets how much harm everyone takes before it tells: chin, brain strain, face and rib fractures, tissue damage, legs giving way, the whole-body knockout, cutting through, killing stabs and blood loss. Lower ends fights sooner with fists and weapons alike; the physics is untouched.
- **Team fights**, 1 to 8 a side and not necessarily even. Each fighter keeps a focus: the man in front of him, the nearest one standing once his man is down, at once whoever hits him instead. Sides line up in rows that fit the floor. Gangs take turns (at most three on one man; the rest circle just out of range), numbers count for confidence, and blows from outside a man's field of view land harder (blindside). Team-mates spread out, sidestep to keep the line clear, hold a punch while a team-mate is in or just past it, and keep kicks for when nobody stands beside them: friendly fire under 2%. `node tools/crowds.js` measures speed, outcomes, friendly fire and glitches. In the builder every fighter on both sides can be picked and edited; **Copy setup** / **Load setup** keep the whole fight (level, sides, every fighter) as JSON.
- **Levels** (`src/scenarios.js`). Set fights in set places, on the same world and physics: the subway level puts two men in street clothes, bare-fisted, on a narrow platform. Bare fists peak harder and break hands and cut sooner; clothes are lofts weighted like the skin under them; hair, hoods and drawstrings swing as pendulums that rest on the body (`src/dangle.js`); a headset is a prop knocked off by the first clean head shot.
- **Outfits** (`src/outfits.js`, drawn by `src/wardrobe.js`). Boxing, Sports, Sumo, Hiking, Casual, Business, Yakuza, SWAT, Knight armour, Samurai armour (red lacquered lamellar, five designs) and Hoplomachus. Traits are physics or harm, kept apart: movement (footwork, acceleration, limb speed), balance, kick weight, gear mass (real mass: knockback follows it), and protection that cuts harm by kind, never the impulse:

  | Armour | Blunt | Cut | Pierce | Weight |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | SWAT | 80% | 60% | 40% | +20% |
  | Knight (blades glance off) | 60% | 100% | 90% | +50% |
  | Samurai | 70% | 90% | 80% | +40% |
  | Hoplomachus | 40% | 40% | 20% | +30% |

  Steel is a separate metal mesh lit by one painted reflection map; lacquered lamellar is less metallic, so its red shows.
- **Weapons** (`src/weapons.js`, drawn by `src/weaponview.js`). Police baton, long sword (hand-and-a-half), katana (two hands, held upright), knife, and the hoplomachus's spear with a parma shield and a gladius in reserve. A weapon is a rigid line from the main hand; a wrist spring turns it as fast as its inertia allows (stiffer with two hands, the off hand drawn onto the grip), and its mass rides on the hands. Weapon moves follow a path through the target. On contact the impulse is a collision of effective masses — a free body, or arm and weapon locked and turning about the shoulder for a cut, or pushing as one for a thrust — and the harm splits by how the contact moved: an edge drawn across cuts, a point arriving first pierces, and the rest is blunt. Cuts bleed and, deep enough near a joint, take the part off (the skin above it is lifted into a loose piece; the stump bleeds in beats; blood stains the floor); a deep stab into the chest or head kills; blood loss collapses a man. Plate turns blades aside; a shield stops what meets it; blades clash; hard blows to the weapon arm or the weapon jar the grip loose, and a man knocked down drops it — then he boxes (the hoplomachus draws his gladius). `node tools/weapon-strikes.js katana` throws each move at a standing man from set distances; `node tools/weapons.js` plays weapon bouts and tallies how they end; `node tools/glitches.js weapons` sweeps them for glitches.
- **Places.** The ring, the subway platform and the Colosseum (sand, the podium wall and its gates, the stepped cavea full of people, open sky and sun) can be picked in the sandbox.
- **Big moments** (`src/drama.js`). Knockouts, severed parts, killing stabs and knockdowns slow the simulation, push the camera in and shake it; icons pop over KOs, cuts through, kills, blood loss, disarms, knockdowns, broken joints and lost gear.
  - Boxers fight in combinations at a volume puncher's pace, and defend by reading the strike: after a reaction time training shortens, the head goes off the punch's line (outside the punching arm, under a hook), and it weaves off the centre line whenever in range. `node tools/aggression.js boxing 16 60` prints punch rate, combination share and how many head shots land clean.
- **Body contact.** Collisions share momentum by mass. A charge transfers its momentum. Blows put a "knock" into the trunk that the legs must absorb, and too much topples a fighter. The clinch locks hands to the opponent's neck with constraints. Leg kicks accumulate damage until the leg gives way.
- **Injuries.** A blow hands over more momentum than its cushioned damage figure, for a sharp knockback. Knees and elbows are hinges, hips and the neck are cones. A joint held well past its range (strain = angle past the limit × time, leaking away) breaks: its limb goes limp and turns red, and a broken knee, hip or neck ends the fight. Each body segment accumulates damage from the blows it takes, and the skin reddens with it.
- **Fight AI** (`src/ai.js`). The AI issues the same commands the player does. It chooses moves by distance and defences by what is coming, and its style sets how often it presses inside or charges.
- **Hits.** A landed punch gives its momentum to the struck part, and the punching arm takes the same momentum back. The struck muscles hold only passive tone for a reflex delay of 55–85 ms, then catch the part with whatever force they have. Muscles are spring-dampers capped by their strength, so every movement has inertia. Knees, elbows and the neck have joint limits for the ragdoll.
- **Face** (`src/face.js`). Lightly anime: large drawn eyes (white, two-tone iris, pupil, highlights, a heavy lash line), a small nose and mouth, and a soft V-shaped jaw. Hair is built from tapered locks (spiky, short, fade, buzz, cornrows, bun, ponytail), and there is optional facial hair. It winces when hit, goes slack when stunned, shuts when knocked out, pants when tired, and reddens with damage.
- **Rig and skin** (`src/rig.js`, `src/bodymesh.js`). Seventeen bones, whose frames come straight from the physics particles. The body is one continuous skinned mesh, generated from that fighter's anatomy: each muscle group and fat deposit is a rounded shape, blended into the next with a smooth minimum. The mesh is built in an A-pose (surface nets) and each vertex is weighted to its nearest bones. A limb's bones only move skin on their own side of the body.
- **Skeleton** (`src/bones.js`). Skull with jaw and eye sockets, vertebrae, ten pairs of ribs and a sternum, shoulder blades, clavicles, pelvis, humerus, radius and ulna, femur and kneecap, tibia and fibula, and foot bones. Each piece rides its rig bone.
- **Style** (`src/toon.js`). Cel shading with ink outlines by default, which keeps the stylised faces out of the uncanny valley. Soft lit shading is available to compare.
- **View** (`src/render.js`). Three.js renders four layers: skin, muscle, bone and the physics skeleton. The skin is a soft shell of damped springs, so punches dent it.

## Run

```sh
npm run serve        # http://127.0.0.1:8934
npm test
npm run bout -- heavy:muayThai light:kickboxing 10 180   # headless bouts by preset:style
npm run bundle       # dist/gladiator.html, one self-contained page
```
